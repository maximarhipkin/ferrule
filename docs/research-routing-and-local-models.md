# Multi-Provider Routing and Runtime Local Models — Research for Ferrule

**Research report — September 24, 2026**

## TL;DR

- **Question A (route across providers) has a mature answer; question B (train a local model at runtime) mostly doesn't — yet.** Learned routing between 2-3 providers is production-proven with public cost numbers. "Create a model while running" is real only in the narrow, well-scoped sense of a tiny classifier or a LoRA adapter trained periodically offline, not literally online.
- **Real numbers on routing:** RouteLLM (LMSYS/Berkeley) cuts cost **>2×** with no quality loss using a router trained on human preference data ([arXiv:2406.18665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665)); FrugalGPT's LLM-cascade matches GPT-4-level quality at **up to 98% lower cost**, or beats GPT-4 accuracy by 4% at the same cost ([arXiv:2305.05176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176)); Hybrid LLM routing (ICLR 2024) cuts large-model calls **up to 40%** with no quality drop ([arXiv:2404.14618](https://huggingface.co/papers/2404.14618)); Microsoft's BEST-Route cuts cost **up to 60%** with **<1%** quality drop by also choosing how many samples to draw ([arXiv:2506.22716](https://github.com/microsoft/best-route-llm)). RouterBench is the standard eval harness: 405,467 samples, 8 datasets, 14 models, scored on a cost-quality plane ([arXiv:2403.12031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12031)).
- **The two real pitfalls are switching cost, not routing accuracy.** Prompt caches are per-model: switching models mid-session forfeits the cached prefix and the very next call re-reads (and re-writes, at the ~1.25-2× cache-write rate) the *entire* history uncached — a documented 10× cost spike on a 50K-token system prompt ([tianpan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/05/24/the-model-migration-that-broke-your-prompt-cache), [Claude Code Docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/prompt-caching)). And tool-call/reasoning formats are not portable — a router that swaps mid-turn breaks Claude's `tool_use`/`cache_control` blocks, OpenAI's Responses `previous_response_id` chain, and Kimi's interleaved `reasoning_content`.
- **On outputs and training, the terms are asymmetric and the exact wording matters.** Anthropic's Commercial ToS: *"Customer may not and must not attempt to... access the Services to build a competing product or service, including to train competing AI models"* — but this is scoped to *competing* models; Anthropic's own help center lists sentiment analysis, categorization, and summarization tools as permitted. OpenAI's Terms of Use bars using *"Output to develop models that compete with OpenAI"* but explicitly **permits** models "primarily intended to categorize, classify, or organize data" if not sold to third parties. Google's Gemini API terms: on **paid** tiers, *"Google doesn't use your prompts... or responses to improve our products"*; on the **free** tier, *"Google uses the content you submit... and any generated responses to provide, improve, and develop Google products"* — and separately, the developer *"may not use the Services to develop models that compete with the Services."* **Inference (not a quoted clause): a tiny, internal, non-distributed routing/tool-selection classifier trained on Ferrule's own transcripts reads as squarely inside every vendor's stated exception — it is not a general-purpose competing chatbot — but this is a plain-English reading, not legal advice, and Max should not take it as one.**
- **Local-model-at-runtime, sized correctly, means three different things**, ranked by how real they are today: (1) periodic (not truly "live") LoRA fine-tunes of a small open model on Ferrule's own transcripts for narrow sub-tasks — routing, tool-name selection, compaction triage; (2) a tiny classifier/kNN over embeddings, retrained cheaply and often, for the same jobs; (3) semantic/prompt caching, which needs **no training at all** and should be the baseline every "local model" idea is measured against. GPTCache-style semantic caching alone reports **61.6-68.8%** cache-hit rates and correspondingly fewer LLM calls in production categories ([arXiv:2411.05276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05276)).
- **Hardware reality check:** LoRA fine-tuning needs a GPU-shaped memory bandwidth profile; Max's CPU-only VPS is the wrong machine for it (training, unlike serving, is compute-bound, not just bandwidth-bound — no primary source measured this specific case, flagged as inference). His M1 Pro MacBook (32GB, ~200GB/s) or a future M-series Mac via **MLX-LM** is the realistic free option: `mlx_lm.lora --model <model> --data ... --iters 1000` fine-tunes an 8B QLoRA in about an hour on a 16GB Mac, with 32GB comfortable for a 12-14B model ([MLX-LM LoRA guides](https://dzone.com/articles/fine-tuning-llms-locally-using-mlx-lm-guide), [Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/lora-fine-tuning-on-your-apple-silicon-macbook-432c7dab614a/)) — full specs and bandwidth numbers cross-checked against `/workspace/agent/max-hardware-decision.md`.
- **Speculative decoding is off the table against every closed API** — it requires running the small "draft" model's forward pass yourself and having the target model return per-token acceptance in the same decoding loop, which none of Anthropic/OpenAI/Moonshot's chat-completions-style APIs expose ([Leviathan et al., arXiv:2211.17192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192); DeepMind, Chen et al.). It only applies to models you run yourself.
- **Biggest first win for Ferrule, concretely:** a rule-based `RouterProvider` (cheap→expensive escalation by task shape, not a learned model) plus the per-call ledger that has to exist before any learned router can be trained. Learned routing and local-model training are both *downstream* of having that ledger; building them first is building on sand.

## 1. Grounding: what Ferrule has today

Ferrule's `Provider` trait (`crates/ferrule-core/src/provider.rs`) is already the right seam for routing — `async fn complete(&self, req: CompletionRequest) -> Result<CompletionResponse, CoreError>`, with `Provider: Send + Sync` and the doc comment "implementations must be cheap to clone (Arc internally)." A `RouterProvider` is just another `impl Provider` that owns a `Vec<Arc<dyn Provider>>` and picks one per call — no core-crate changes required, no new trait needed. `CompletionResponse.usage` already carries `input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `cached_input_tokens` (`ferrule-core::message::Usage`), which is most of a cost ledger's raw material already flowing through every call.

The one concrete provider today, `OpenAiCompatProvider` (`ferrule-providers/src/openai_compat.rs`), targets the OpenAI chat-completions wire format and already reads both cache-accounting shapes (`prompt_tokens_details.cached_tokens` and `prompt_cache_hit_tokens`) and `reasoning_content` for interleaved-thinking models — so it covers Kimi, DeepSeek, OpenRouter, Groq, and any local server (Ollama/llama.cpp/vLLM) speaking the same dialect for free. There is no Claude Messages API driver and no Codex Responses API driver yet (both are `docs/research-report.md` roadmap items) — which matters directly for routing, because a router that only ever sees `OpenAiCompatProvider` instances can't yet exploit Claude's `cache_control` breakpoints or Codex's `previous_response_id` reasoning retention; it would be routing between behaviorally-flattened providers, not their native capability.

`ferrule-memory` (SQLite, FTS5 BM25 + 7-day-half-life time decay) already reserves an `embedding BLOB` column on the `memories` table that is unused today — that column is the natural home for a routing-relevant embedding index (option B.2 below) without a new crate. `transcript.rs` is an append-only per-session JSONL log (`Message` records plus free-text `Event` records) — the natural place to also append routing decisions and their outcomes, which is exactly what a training-data source for a learned router or a fine-tuned classifier would need to become.

PLAN.md's own gap list (Current State, updated 2026-09-24) already names both pieces this report is about as open: "no cost/observability ledger" and no answer yet for how a router or local model would fit. This report proposes closing the ledger gap first, deliberately, because everything else in both research questions depends on it.

## 2. Question A — multi-provider routing

### 2.1 The taxonomy, from cheapest-to-build to most-research

**Static/rule-based failover** — a fixed priority list, retried on error or rate-limit, no learning. This is what LiteLLM's `Router` does by default: a model-group fallback chain (`order=1 → order=2 → ...`), immediate cooldown on HTTP 429, and a "weighted failover" mode that first re-picks within the same group before escalating across groups ([LiteLLM routing docs](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/routing-load-balancing), [LiteLLM reliability docs](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/proxy/reliability)). OpenRouter and Portkey occupy the same tier commercially — a gateway in front of N providers with static or lightly-configured routing rules. This tier requires zero training data and is deployable in an afternoon; it just can't adapt to query difficulty.

**Cascades** — try cheap first, escalate only on low confidence. FrugalGPT is the reference implementation: query the cheapest LLM API first, and if a small trained "scoring" model judges the answer unreliable, pass the same query to the next-more-expensive model, and so on ([arXiv:2305.05176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176)). The reported numbers are the strongest in this whole space: matching GPT-4-level quality at **up to 98% lower cost**, or **+4% accuracy over GPT-4 at equal cost**. The mechanism generalizes cleanly to Ferrule: cheap model attempts the turn, a deterministic or learned gate (tool-call malformed, confidence low, task tagged "hard" by a keyword/regex heuristic) triggers a retry on the next tier up.

**Learned pre-routers** — decide the model *before* calling anything, from the query alone. RouteLLM trains a router on human preference data (e.g. "would a human prefer the strong or weak model's answer to this query") and reports **>2× cost reduction with no quality loss**, plus a finding directly relevant to Ferrule's multi-provider ambition: the trained routers **transfer** — they keep working when the underlying strong/weak models are swapped out at test time, so the router doesn't need retraining every time a provider ships a new model ([arXiv:2406.18665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665), [LMSYS blog](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2024-07-01-routellm/)). Hybrid LLM (ICLR 2024) is the same idea with a tunable quality/cost dial at inference time — the desired quality level is a runtime parameter, not baked into training — and reports **up to 40% fewer large-model calls at no quality drop** ([arXiv:2404.14618](https://huggingface.co/papers/2404.14618)). Microsoft's BEST-Route extends the decision from "which model" to "which model, and how many samples" and reports **up to 60% cost reduction at <1% performance drop** ([arXiv:2506.22716](https://github.com/microsoft/best-route-llm)).

**Parallel best-of-n / judge / mixture-of-agents** — call multiple providers for the same query and pick or merge. This is the most expensive tier (you pay for every branch) and is not covered by a dedicated primary source in this research pass beyond its presence as a category in Anthropic's own pattern taxonomy ("orchestrator-workers", "evaluator-optimizer" — [Anthropic, Building Effective Agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents), already cited in `docs/research-report.md` §3.4). It is the right tool only when a wrong answer is much more expensive than 2-3× the inference cost — not a default.

**The benchmark that ties it together:** RouterBench evaluates all of the above on a shared cost-quality plane across 405,467 samples, 8 datasets, and 14 models (Llama-70B, Mixtral, Claude variants, GPT-4/3.5, etc.), and is the closest thing this field has to a standard eval — worth adopting directly rather than inventing a bespoke Ferrule benchmark ([arXiv:2403.12031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12031), code at [github.com/withmartian/routerbench](https://github.com/withmartian/routerbench)).

### 2.2 How existing coding agents actually do it

**Aider** splits the *role*, not the query, into two models: an "Architect" model reasons about the code change, and a separate, usually cheaper "Editor" model turns that reasoning into well-formed diffs — because editors are more sensitive to strict output-formatting instructions than reasoning is ([aider.chat/2024/09/26/architect.html](https://aider.chat/2024/09/26/architect.html)). It also uses a third, deliberately weak/cheap model just for commit messages and chat-history summarization. This is a routing-by-*subtask*, not routing-by-query-difficulty, pattern — directly applicable to Ferrule's compaction and diary-writing paths, which don't need a frontier model.

**Claude Code** applies routing-by-subtask at the subagent level: its built-in `Explore` subagent (file search, grep, simple lookups) runs on Haiku, which is reported as roughly **15× cheaper per token than Opus**, reserving Opus for turns where a wrong answer is more expensive than a slower one ([Claude Code subagent docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents); cost-ratio and pattern corroborated by independent write-ups, e.g. [jsmanifest](https://jsmanifest.com/claude-model-routing-subagents)). This is a static/rule-based router by *task type*, configured by a human once, not learned — closer to FrugalGPT's cheap-first cascade philosophy than to RouteLLM's learned classifier.

**OpenClaw/NanoClaw**, per `docs/research-report.md` §4.4 (already researched in this repo), handle multi-provider at the *agent-group* configuration level rather than inside a single request loop — each agent gets a fixed provider, and provider diversity is a deployment-time choice, not a per-call routing decision. That report's Provider comparison table (§4.4) is worth reusing as-is for a `HarnessProfile`-aware `RouterProvider`.

### 2.3 Signals that actually work

Across the sources above, the routing signals with published evidence are: (1) a **learned classifier over the query text** trained on preference data (RouteLLM) or difficulty labels (Hybrid LLM) — needs a labeled dataset, the single biggest practical barrier; (2) **task-type / subtask tagging**, rule-based, zero training data (Aider, Claude Code) — the cheapest signal and the one Ferrule can ship first, since `ferrule-core`'s loop already distinguishes tool-call turns from plain-text turns and could distinguish "compaction pass" from "user turn" trivially; (3) **confidence/verification on the cheap model's own output** (FrugalGPT's scoring step, "escalate on malformed tool call or empty response") — cheap to implement, needs no training data, and composes with (2); (4) **historical outcome per (task-type, provider) pair** — not covered by name in any single source here, but it is the direct implication of RouteLLM's transfer-learning finding: since a router trained on preference data keeps working across model swaps, an *outcome ledger* keyed by task shape rather than by specific model name is the durable signal, and is exactly what §4's ledger proposal captures.

### 2.4 Pitfalls

- **Prompt-cache loss on model switch.** Caches are scoped per model; switching mid-session forfeits the cached prefix, and the next call re-reads (and re-writes, at a premium — reported as 1.25× on a 5-minute TTL, 2× on a 1-hour TTL) the entire history uncached. A real-world example: a 50,000-token system prompt at 90% cache hit rate effectively costs ~5,000 tokens/request; the day after an uncoordinated model migration, it costs the full 50,000 — a **10× cost spike from routing alone**, with zero change in task difficulty ([tianpan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/05/24/the-model-migration-that-broke-your-prompt-cache), [Claude Code prompt-caching docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/prompt-caching)). **Implication for Ferrule: a `RouterProvider` must route per-*session*, not per-*call*, or must accept that every route switch is a full-price turn** — cascades that escalate mid-session are the expensive case; escalating at session start is cheap.
- **Tool-call and reasoning-format incompatibility.** Anthropic's `tool_use`/`tool_result` content blocks, OpenAI's Responses `previous_response_id` reasoning-retention chain, and Kimi's `reasoning_content` interleaved-thinking field are three different wire dialects for the same concept (`docs/research-report.md` §4, already researched in this repo). `OpenAiCompatProvider::to_wire` already round-trips `reasoning_content` — but a router that hands a half-finished tool-call turn from a Claude-dialect provider to an OpenAI-dialect provider has no defined behavior today; the `Message`/`ToolCall` core types are dialect-neutral, but the *history* of a turn is not portable mid-turn across dialects without a translation layer that doesn't exist yet.
- **Off-distribution routers.** Not covered by a dedicated primary source in this pass, but it's the direct converse of RouteLLM's headline transfer-learning result: a router *validated* to transfer across model swaps was still trained on some preference distribution — a router trained on general chat preference data has no demonstrated guarantee on Ferrule's actual traffic shape (tool-heavy agentic turns, not chat Q&A). This is a real gap between the published numbers and Ferrule's use case, not something the sources resolve — flagged as an open risk, not dismissed.

## 3. Question B — a local model created/trained at runtime

### 3.1 What "at runtime" can honestly mean

Read literally, "create a model while the agent is running" is not something any of the primary sources here describe as production practice — training even a small model is a batch job (data collection → train → eval → deploy), not a request-time operation. The honest decomposition, from least to most literal:

1. **Periodic LoRA fine-tunes**, run on a schedule (Ferrule already has a scheduler — M3, shipped 2026-09-24) against accumulated transcript data, producing a new small-model checkpoint every N days/weeks. This is "runtime" in the sense that the *agent itself* triggers and owns the training job, not a human running a notebook — but each individual inference call still hits a static, already-trained checkpoint.
2. **A tiny non-LLM classifier or kNN over embeddings**, retrained far more cheaply and often (minutes, not hours) than an LLM fine-tune, for narrow routing/gating decisions.
3. **Caching** — not a model at all, but the thing every "local model" proposal needs to be measured against, because it captures the same "make repeat/similar work cheaper" goal with zero training risk.

### 3.2 Option 1 — LoRA/distill a small open model on Ferrule's own transcripts

LoRA freezes the base model's weights and trains small rank-decomposition adapter matrices instead, reducing trainable parameters by up to **10,000×** and GPU memory by **3×** versus full fine-tuning, with **no added inference latency** because the adapter can be merged back into the base weights ([Hu et al., arXiv:2106.09685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)). This is the right tool for Ferrule's narrow sub-tasks named in the brief — routing, tool-name selection, compaction triage, recall reranking, gate decisions — because these are all *classification-shaped* or *short-generation-shaped* tasks with a small, well-defined output space, exactly where a small fine-tuned model competes with a frontier model at a fraction of the cost.

**Data volume.** None of the sources in this pass give a hard "N examples needed" number specific to routing/tool-selection LoRA; the closest concrete anchor is general LoRA-fine-tuning guidance recommending **200-500 labeled examples** as a practical starting point for a narrow task via `mlx_lm.lora` ([DZone MLX-LM guide](https://dzone.com/articles/fine-tuning-llms-locally-using-mlx-lm-guide)) — flagged as a rule-of-thumb from a practitioner guide, not a controlled study. Ferrule's own JSONL transcripts (`transcript.rs`) plus a routing-outcome ledger (§4) are the exact right shape of data source, once enough sessions have accumulated — which argues for building the ledger now even before there's enough data to train on.

**Training time/cost, CPU VPS vs Apple Silicon vs rented GPU.** No primary source in this pass benchmarks LoRA training throughput on a CPU-only VPS specifically — flagged as inference, not measured: training is compute-bound (many forward+backward passes over the same data) rather than the memory-bandwidth-bound regime that dominates *inference* token generation, so Max's CPU-only Linux VPS (per this project's own environment notes) is very likely impractical for this, plausibly an order of magnitude or more slower than any GPU-class device — but this report did not find a source measuring that gap directly and it should be verified empirically (a 10-minute timed LoRA run on the VPS vs a Mac) before being treated as fact. What *is* sourced: on Apple Silicon via MLX-LM, `mlx_lm.lora --model <model> --data ... --iters 1000` runs QLoRA fine-tuning directly, an 8B-parameter QLoRA finishes in about an hour on a 16GB Mac, and 32GB is described as the practical sweet spot for a useful 12-14B model with headroom ([Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/lora-fine-tuning-on-your-apple-silicon-macbook-432c7dab614a/), [DZone](https://dzone.com/articles/fine-tuning-llms-locally-using-mlx-lm-guide)). Cross-referenced against `/workspace/agent/max-hardware-decision.md`: Max's current MacBook Pro (M1 Pro, 32GB, ~200GB/s memory bandwidth) sits right at that "32GB sweet spot," so it is capable of exactly this kind of small-model LoRA job today, no purchase required — a materially different conclusion from that file's local-*inference* framing (Gemma 27B at 5-8 tok/s), because a routing/tool-selection LoRA target model would be far smaller (1-3B class) than a general-chat local model. A rented GPU (e.g. a cloud A10/L4-class instance) is the fallback if transcript volume grows past what a laptop session can comfortably train in an evening; no primary pricing source was pulled for this in this pass.

### 3.3 Option 2 — tiny online non-LLM models (embedding + classifier/kNN)

This is cheaper and more "runtime" than LoRA in every sense that matters: retraining a logistic-regression or kNN classifier over embeddings on a few hundred new labeled routing decisions takes seconds to minutes, not an hour-plus GPU/Mac session, so it can genuinely run on a schedule far tighter than a weekly LoRA pass — plausibly after every N task runs. `ferrule-memory`'s existing but unused `embedding BLOB` column is the direct implementation seam: embed each transcript's query, store the routing decision and its outcome (§4) alongside it, and do a kNN lookup ("what did similar past queries route to, and how did that turn out") as the router's first, cheapest signal, falling back to the rule-based tier (§2.1) when there isn't enough history yet. No dedicated primary source in this pass benchmarks this exact pattern for LLM routing specifically, but it is a direct, low-risk application of RouteLLM's core finding (a small trained signal beats a fixed rule) at a fraction of LoRA's training cost.

### 3.4 Option 3 — semantic/prompt caching as the baseline to beat

Any "train a local model to make things faster and cheaper" proposal should be justified against caching first, because caching needs **zero training** and already reports strong numbers: GPT Semantic Cache-style embedding caching reports **61.6-68.8%** cache-hit rates across production query categories, with correspondingly reduced LLM API calls and latency ([arXiv:2411.05276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05276)). Separately, and more directly actionable for Ferrule today: **provider-side prompt caching** (the `cache_control`-breakpoint mechanism already discussed in `docs/research-report.md` §3.2/§4.1) requires no new infrastructure at all, just a stable system prefix — and per §2.4 above, is actively *endangered* by naive routing, not helped by it. The practical ordering for Ferrule is: ship stable-prefix prompt caching and session-scoped routing first (near-zero engineering cost, protects an existing win), then semantic caching for repeated/similar queries (moderate cost — needs the embedding infra §3.3 already wants), and only then consider training anything.

### 3.5 Speculative decoding — ruled out against closed APIs

Speculative decoding uses a small "draft" model to propose several tokens autoregressively, then verifies all of them against the larger "target" model in a single forward pass, accepting the ones that match and re-sampling from the first mismatch — a **2-3×** wall-clock speedup reported in the original work on T5-XXL, and **2-2.5×** on a 70B Chinchilla model in DeepMind's independent formulation ([Leviathan et al., arXiv:2211.17192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192); Chen et al., DeepMind, cited via [Google Research retrospective](https://research.google/blog/looking-back-at-speculative-decoding/)). This mechanically **requires white-box access to the target model's per-token logits inside the same decoding loop as the draft model** — something no chat-completions-style API (Anthropic, OpenAI, Moonshot) exposes. It is real, well-evidenced, and completely inapplicable to Ferrule's actual provider mix unless Ferrule is also serving the target model itself (e.g. speeding up a locally-hosted model via llama.cpp/mistral.rs with its own local draft model) — worth keeping on the roadmap purely for the local-model-serving path, not for anything involving Claude/GPT/Kimi over the network.

### 3.6 Eval/regression guards and privacy

No dedicated primary source in this pass covers eval/regression-guard methodology specific to a fine-tuned routing model; the closest grounded anchor is `docs/research-report.md`'s already-researched Google "Industrial Agentic Engineering" principle that **"the build system is truth"** — deterministic verifiers over model self-judgment — which generalizes directly here: a routing/tool-selection LoRA or classifier should be gated on a held-out slice of real transcript outcomes (did the escalation actually happen when needed, did the cheap-tier answer actually match a verifier), not on subjective review, before being promoted from "candidate" to "live," mirroring the `[agent] verify_command` pattern Ferrule already has for code changes. On privacy: training only on Ferrule's own transcripts (never on raw provider outputs sent to a third party, and never leaving the machine it trains on) sidesteps essentially all of the ToS concerns in the TL;DR — the risk there is about *what a vendor lets you do with their model's output*, not about running your own small model on your own data on your own hardware, which is uncontested in every source pulled here.

**Inference, clearly separated from the quotes above:** a small internal classifier/LoRA adapter trained on Ferrule's own transcripts, for routing/tool-selection/compaction decisions, used only inside Ferrule and never sold or distributed — is a narrow, non-competing, internal-tooling use case. None of the three vendors' quoted language squarely prohibits this. This is a plain reading for planning purposes, not a legal opinion, and Max should treat it as a decision input, not a decision.

### 3.8 Rust implementation paths

Two shapes, both real, with a clear cost/effort ordering:

- **Behind an OpenAI-compatible endpoint (llama.cpp server / Ollama / mistral.rs's own server mode).** Ollama serves `/v1/chat/completions`, `/v1/embeddings`, and related routes on the same port as its native API, no API key required ([Ollama OpenAI compatibility docs](https://docs.ollama.com/api/openai-compatibility)). **`OpenAiCompatProvider` already speaks this dialect** — pointing it at `http://localhost:11434/v1` with any model name Ollama has pulled is a config change, not a code change. This is the fastest path to "Ferrule can call a local model" and should be the first thing built, regardless of whether that local model is ever fine-tuned.
- **In-process via Rust bindings** — `candle` (HuggingFace's minimalist Rust ML framework, GPU-capable via CUDA/Metal, wide model coverage including Llama/Mistral/Phi/Gemma — [huggingface/candle](https://github.com/huggingface/candle)), `mistral.rs` (built on top of Candle, positioned as a high-performance pure-Rust inference engine — [mistral.rs comparison writeups](https://medium.com/@zaiinn440/apple-mlx-vs-llama-cpp-vs-hugging-face-candle-rust-for-lightning-fast-llms-locally-5447f6e9255a)), or `llama-cpp` Rust bindings, or `ort` (ONNX Runtime bindings, better fit for a small classifier exported to ONNX than for a full LLM). In-process avoids a subprocess/HTTP hop and lets Ferrule embed the tiny classifier from §3.3 directly, but it is real new dependency surface and a new `Provider`/tool implementation to maintain — worth it only once the OpenAI-compatible path has proven the local-model idea is worth keeping.

## 4. Phased proposal for Ferrule

### Phase 0 — the ledger (build this first, before any routing logic)

Add a `RoutingOutcome` record, appended to the session's existing JSONL transcript (or a sibling file) alongside each `Message`/`Event`: `{ provider, model, task_shape, input_tokens, output_tokens, cached_input_tokens, latency_ms, outcome }`, where `task_shape` is a cheap static tag (e.g. "scheduler turn", "tool-heavy chat turn", "compaction pass") and `outcome` is whatever cheap signal already exists (did the turn error, did it need a retry, did compaction actually shrink the transcript). This uses data `CompletionResponse.usage` already produces — the gap is only that nothing persists it today (PLAN.md's own gap list: "no cost/observability ledger"). This is small, additive, and touches no existing trait signature.

### Phase 1 — `RouterProvider`, rule-based

```
pub struct RouterProvider {
    tiers: Vec<Arc<dyn Provider>>,   // cheapest first
    escalate_on: EscalationPolicy,   // malformed tool call, error, empty response, task_shape tag
}
```
Implements `Provider` exactly like any other backend (`name()`, `complete()`), so nothing above it in `ferrule-core::Agent` needs to change — this is the direct payoff of the trait already being narrow. Route **per session, not per call** (§2.4) to protect prompt caching; escalate on the FrugalGPT-style cheap tier: a malformed/empty response or an explicit "hard" tag triggers a retry one tier up, logged to the Phase-0 ledger. No training data needed to ship this.

### Phase 2 — learned router, once the ledger has data

Once Phase 0 has weeks of real (task_shape, provider, outcome) rows, train a small classifier (§3.3 — kNN/logistic regression over embeddings, not an LLM) on that data, following RouteLLM's proven shape but on Ferrule's own agentic-turn distribution rather than borrowed chat-preference data (closing the off-distribution gap flagged in §2.4). This is the option-2 "tiny online non-LLM model," retrainable in minutes as more data accumulates, and it plugs into `RouterProvider`'s `EscalationPolicy` as an additional signal ahead of the rule-based fallback.

### Phase 3 — narrow LoRA fine-tunes for specific sub-tasks

Only once Phase 2's classifier data shows a specific, high-volume, narrow sub-task (tool-name selection is the likely first candidate, given how mechanical it is) where a fine-tuned small open model would plausibly beat both the cheap-tier provider's raw accuracy and the classifier's coverage. Train via `mlx_lm.lora` on Max's Mac (§3.2), evaluate against the Phase-0 ledger's real outcomes (§3.6), and serve through an OpenAI-compatible local server (§3.8) — no in-process Rust ML bindings needed at this stage.

### The single biggest first win

**Phase 0 plus a two-tier Phase-1 router (cheap default model, escalate to today's default on error/complexity tag) for the scheduler's non-interactive task turns** — this is where Ferrule already knows the task shape in advance (`ferrule tasks add`), already has no live human waiting on the reply, and is the lowest-risk place to test escalation logic without touching interactive chat latency. It requires zero new dependencies, zero training data, and is directly testable against the M3 scheduler's existing truthful run-status logging.

### What's hype

- **"Train a model while the agent runs"** taken literally — no source in this pass describes literal request-time training; every real system either fine-tunes on a schedule or uses a non-LLM classifier that trains in seconds. Ferrule should describe this feature to Max as "the agent periodically improves a small helper model from its own history," not "a model that learns live."
- **Speculative decoding as a routing feature** — it's real, but it's a *local-serving* optimization with no purchase on any of Ferrule's actual (closed-API) providers today (§3.5).
- **Parallel best-of-n/mixture-of-agents as a default** — real pattern, no source in this pass shows it beating a well-tuned two-tier cascade on cost-adjusted quality; it's a tool for specific high-stakes decisions, not a routing default.
- **A learned router as the *first* thing to build** — RouteLLM's own numbers depend on a training set; skipping straight to a learned router with no ledger and no data is building the expensive piece before the cheap piece that makes it possible.

### Decisions for Max

1. **Escalation policy for Phase 1** — what counts as "hard enough to escalate" for the first cascade (malformed tool call only? explicit task-shape tags? both?) — a product call, not a research one.
2. **Which providers become tiers** — Ferrule today only has one working driver (`OpenAiCompatProvider`, i.e. Kimi/DeepSeek/OpenRouter/local); a real cost-tiered cascade needs at least a second, cheaper-vs-pricier pair live (e.g. Kimi K2.6 cheap tier + Claude/GPT expensive tier) before Phase 1 is worth building at all.
3. **Whether the "local model" framing should be scoped down** in what Ferrule tells Max/users it does, per "What's hype" above — a periodic small-model refresh vs. a live-training claim.
4. **Hardware for Phase 3** — confirmed usable today (Max's M1 Pro, 32GB) per `max-hardware-decision.md`, but it competes with his own dev workload; worth deciding now whether Phase 3 fine-tunes run on his laptop, a rented GPU, or wait for a hardware upgrade, rather than deciding it under time pressure later.
5. **Legal comfort with the §3.7 inference** — whether Max wants his own read of the ToS carve-outs confirmed by counsel before Phase 3 ships, given it's this report's own plain-English interpretation, not a quoted permission.
